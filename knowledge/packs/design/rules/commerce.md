# Commerce Design Contract

Use when the brief describes an online store, shopping cart, or purchase flow. Apply it to the agreed page scope; a homepage does not need to duplicate a full catalog or checkout.

## Block plan

For a store homepage, propose at least one visible product shopping section, a clear path to the full catalog, and an accessible cart entry. Keep editorial storytelling, sold-item archives, and custom-order services as distinct blocks when the brief calls for them. Explain how each block supports discovery, purchase, trust, or brand identity. Review the order for sales clarity and visual rhythm with the designer before drawing layouts.

If the designer asks to omit a shopping block or add a block that could weaken the primary purchase path, describe the trade-off and an alternative, then follow the resolved decision. Do not silently reinterpret “online store” as a portfolio.

## Product cards and demo data

Show product image, meaningful product name, displayed price with currency, a path to details, and a visible add-to-cart action when direct purchase is part of the agreed flow. Include size or variant choice and availability only when relevant; show how the action works when a choice is required. A photo with a generic “Look 01” caption and “Details” link alone is a gallery item, not a product card.

Use supplied product data when available. Otherwise create coherent demonstration names, short descriptions, suitable images, and plausible prices in the agreed currency. Record that these are synthetic in Figma annotations and the handoff, and list the fields that need replacement before publication. Do not imply a demo item is genuinely in stock, sold out, or available in a specific size. If the image is from an archive, do not present it as a purchasable item without confirmation; use a generated or clearly labeled demo image instead.

## Icons and shopping controls

Use a recognizable vector/component cart icon with an intentional count treatment. Use real vector/component icons for purchase actions where the visual language calls for them; do not substitute icon names, emoji, or Unicode glyphs for icons. Label icon-only controls for accessibility in the handoff.

## Final check

Inspect the screenshot and trace the primary path: identify a product, see its price, understand how to buy it, and reach the cart. Check that archive items and bespoke services cannot be mistaken for ready-to-buy products. A catalog link alone does not pass this check.
